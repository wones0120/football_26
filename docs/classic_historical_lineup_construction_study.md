# Historical DraftKings NFL Classic lineup construction study

Research date: October 4, 2026. Success means **top 10% of entries in each Classic contest**, with cutoff ties included, as clarified by the user. Cashing is outside this study. First place, top 0.1%, top 1%, and the full stored field are comparison populations.

The study inventories 39 registered contest fields and analyzes 15 verified Classic fields across 10 distinct slates. They contain 2,419,672 entries, of which 2,416,683 have classifiable legal rosters. The primary population contains 242,171 top 10% entries. The report describes their construction and compares it with the field; it does not evaluate an optimizer design.

This is a convenience sample of archived contests, not a representative sample of all DraftKings tournaments. Contest fees, entry limits, and payout shape are unknown for these 15 fields, so a GPP-only or single-entry/3-max/MME claim cannot be certified. No optimizer rules, weights, defaults, projections, or UI behavior were changed. No Week 4 lineups were generated.

Exclusions from structural denominators: 2,936 invalid/blank rosters, 53 rosters with unresolved or ambiguous salary identities, 0 repeated-player rosters, and 0 verified over-cap rosters. Score-sum audit among classified field entries: 1,498,163 reconciled within 0.05 DK points, 918,520 lacked a complete player-score sum, and 0 disagreed. The raw cohorts and weighted unresolved tokens remain available in each contest JSON.

Top-percentile membership uses `rank <= ceil(field_size × fraction)` from the full stored field. This includes all ties at the boundary rather than selecting an arbitrary number of tied entries. The nested populations overlap. Valid Classic slots are QB, two RB, three WR, TE, FLEX, and DST, with nine distinct resolved identities; salary is checked when all nine values are known.

Entry weighting counts every contest entry. Unique weighting counts each unordered nine-player identity set once within each contest, including entries that rearrange interchangeable slots. It does not count duplicated entries as independent football constructions. The same roster may appear in several contests on a slate; those contests are dependent, which is why uncertainty resamples whole slates. Unique roster counts across contests are not asserted to be globally unique.

Extra copies beyond the first instance of each roster account for 13.24% of top 10% entries versus 8.52% of classified field entries. This measures repeated constructions within contests; it is not the fraction of entries belonging to any duplicated roster or a measure of winnings after splits.

Pooled lift is top-percentile pattern frequency divided by field pattern frequency on available observations. Equal-contest lift divides the mean contest top-percentile frequency by the mean corresponding field frequency; it is not the mean of contest lifts. Slate-balanced lift averages contests within each slate and then weights slates equally. Reported 95% intervals use 3,000 bootstrap draws of the whole slate with seed 20261004; fewer than five covered slates have no interval. These are exploratory intervals, without multiple-comparison correction. Millions of entries do not turn ten football slates into millions of independent trials.

The additional Mantel–Haenszel odds ratio compares the odds of placing in the top 10% for a pattern versus other constructions **within the same contest and QB**. Its interval also resamples slates. This helps separate construction patterns from selecting a particular successful QB, but it does not control all player choices, salaries, ownership, injuries, or entrant skill. It is an association, not a causal optimizer parameter.

The prior-volume proxy uses the same earlier-game history: at least 15 carries/game for RB or seven targets/game for WR/TE, and a verified underdog spread. It is not a forecast of current-week volume. The standalone projection proxy uses an archived pre-lock model reference, ranks all available players within natural position, and includes ties at rank ten. A complete singleton group is required for the count; otherwise it is unavailable. A low salary does not establish value without a credible projection, and low ownership does not itself establish leverage. Injuries, opportunity changes, opposing chalk, and individual player quality cannot be inferred from lineup structure alone. Consequently the question whether non-stack players were simply the strongest standalone plays has a limited model-proxy answer, not a definitive historical explanation.

For counts that classify singleton players by market or usage, a roster with no singleton players has a known count of zero even if other games on its slate lack that input. This does not fill any missing player market or usage value. Lineups with actual singleton players require complete relevant coverage.

## Limitations and next evidence needed

The most consequential limits are ten independent slates, nonrandom archive availability, unknown contest categories, one small contest, one three-game contest, and pre-lock markets available on only three slates. A valid top 10% association can reflect which QB/game succeeded, selected player quality, salary allocations, ownership, entrant skill, or injury information. The within-QB diagnostic narrows one confounder but does not remove the others. First-place evidence has only fifteen contest observations and repeated football slates.

For unresolved structural rosters, the primary tables use complete cases. Missingness is explicitly retained by contest and token. For a fully observed binary metric, a worst-case raw-field frequency lies between `known_hits/raw_population_N` and `(known_hits + unclassified_entries)/raw_population_N`; those bounds are exported for focal patterns. Market, ownership, projection, and usage tables have their own available-case denominators and may have much larger missingness. Their availability is not silently converted to false.

A stronger follow-up study would need verified contest metadata and date/slate linkage for the unscoped archive; more independently dated Classic fields across seasons and slate sizes; broad immutable pre-lock market/projection/role captures; and a preregistered set of hypotheses evaluated on held-out later slates. This report makes no promotion recommendation and changes no production rule.
